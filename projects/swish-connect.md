As president of FRC Team 6479 (AZTECHS) and lead of the Innovation Challenge, I led a seven-person team through the 2021 FIRST Global Innovation Challenge, where teams were asked to design a solution that helps a community achieve optimum physical and mental health through movement. Out of 850 applicants worldwide, our project — **Swish! Connect** — was named a Top 20 finalist and represented the state of Arizona.

## The problem

While ideating, we thought about special education students in our own district, including a student named Calvin who struggled to get a basketball into a standard hoop on his own. Digging into the research, we found that special education students only reach up to 55% motor skill mastery by age 20, and that school-district physical therapists are stretched thin — in the Tempe Union High School District, one PT covers seven schools. The Individuals with Disabilities Education Act serves approximately 7.3 million students in the U.S., about 14% of all public school students, so this was a problem with real scale.

## The solution

Swish! Connect is a system with three components: an adjustable robotic basketball hoop, a companion app, and a website.

- **The hoop.** Mounted on two colored aluminum rods with linear bearings, the hoop slides left and right based on the accuracy of the user's shot. As shots get more accurate, the hoop's range of motion shrinks, gently pushing the student toward greater precision — the target is 70% shot accuracy.
- **The app.** A phone or tablet mounted to the backboard uses its camera to track the ball's trajectory in real time, adjust the hoop's movement, and log progress. The app is designed for the student — applause for motivation, and the ability to set their own adjustment levels rather than following predetermined ones.
- **The website.** Aggregates data over time so both students and physical therapists can chart progress and adjust therapy techniques. It also recommends related motor-skill activities (like tying shoes) based on the data.

## My role

As Innovation Challenge lead, I owned the project end-to-end: framing the problem, coordinating the seven-person team through an entirely virtual season on Zoom, and shaping the business pitch, technical explanation, and final presentation delivered to the FIRST judges. I also led outreach to Dr. Hani Ben Amor, director of the ASU Interactive Robotics Laboratory, to partner on the prototype — specifically, the computer vision needed to detect and track the ball mid-flight.

## Outcome

We were named a **Top 20 finalist out of 850 teams worldwide**, and won the "Who Wants to Be an Innovator" game show at the FIRST Global Innovation Awards. The project moved beyond the competition into a real development track: prototyping with ASU and planning for testing within the Tempe Union High School District.
