It is such an interesting time to be learning software engineering. The rapid changes in the field due to AI have totally re-ignited my interest in it!

## My Experiences and Bias

But let me take a step back. Confession: I had actually lost a bit of interest in software engineering for a couple years. Throughout school, I did a lot of software-engineering-adjacent projects. We had hackathons of course, the Technovation challenge, an elective class called SEARCH where we prototyped our own app and presented it at LinkedIn, even the FRC Global Innovation Challenge. They all had the same fundamental process where we had to choose a problem, research and understand our users, design with our users' needs in mind, make sound architectural decisions, and be able to defend our technical choices. Perhaps now that I'm writing it out, these experiences were beyond just software engineering, since we had to come up with our own ideas, work in a team, and pitch it, but software engineering was a big part of it. As a result, by the time I graduated high school, I had a narrow view of what software engineering is and wanted to just take a break from it. The rapid prototyping loop, having to juggle so many different aspects, and the fact that none of them moved into the real world - all of that made me tired. I wanted to just broaden my technical skills. 

So that's what I did! A quick look at my resume shows that I focused on academic research, first in programmable matter and then in applied AI. And I've loved it. Research is definitely something I want to continue doing in any capacity that I can.

## The Pivot

But I'm in a kind of transitional stage in my life. As I'm taking a step back and reflecting on where I am and where I want to go, software engineering feels exciting again. One of the biggest things is that I now have a solid 24/7 partner that can brainstorm with me or implement specific functionalities whenever I need it or help me debug when I hit a wall. The cognitive burden of having to start from scratch on every single aspect is not there anymore. There's a reliable tool that I can turn to! And as of now, I've been relying on Claude.

And the other thing is, what even is software engineering? I think I've put myself in a box where I've assumed software engineering is the same thing as product development. It's not! And with several open-source LLMs that have such broad capabilities, the possibilities on what can be achieved are endless. 

At this point, funnily enough, my biggest interest now is to go back to rapid prototyping. Except this time, I just want to hack things up. I want to focus on creating. I think it wasn't the software engineering itself that I had become tired of, but it was everything around it: pitching, creating a plan, presenting, writing. Engineering, at its heart, is putting the pieces together to achieve a goal. It's almost like solving a puzzle. The puzzles that I want to start with are little things or hurdles that I face. Instead of finding a solution (like an app or a plugin), I'll just make it myself! And in that process, I'll learn the fundamentals again and get up-to-date on today's tech stack. 

## So what's next?

The biggest skill I'm aiming for is creating something that is shippable and could reliably be used by others. It doesn't have to actually be a product, though. There's already such a saturation out there that I don't want to focus on marketing or hitting 1,000 users by Q2. BUT, what I do want is that if a friend saw me using this cool thing and wanted to use it too, I could just give them a link and they'd be able to set themselves up. That means I need to get comfortable again with UI development and backend management (hosting, databases), along with learning for the first time - LLM integration. To what extent will an LLM be involved, I'm not sure yet. Some say that a lot of logic can be absorbed by an LLM. I want to figure that out and earn that as a skill too. 

I have some ideas on what I want to make (shh, can't let them out just yet), and I'm really excited. Here's to constant creation!
